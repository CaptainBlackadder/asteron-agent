Why AI alone will not win

Four of five benchmarked rivals already run AI across the customer lifecycle. Deploying it buys back the ten-point experience gap — it does not create advantage.

Product quality already ranks top three. What competitors cannot copy quickly is a dealer network restored to health — and service and parts carry 52% of dealer profit while only 46% of owners stay past year three.
AI is table stakes. Dealer economics is the wedge.
2

Three choices

Concentrate  The 20 largest cities, SUV and EV, digital-origin demand — about 38% of volume carrying nearly all the share loss.

Sequence  Year 1 runs three tracks at once: a unit mechanism (metro funnel and SLA), a margin mechanism (aftersales), and foundations.

Gate  No rupee moves without a named owner, a baseline and an adoption target — the three tests 43%, 36% and 34% of today's portfolio fails.

Where the units are lost

2.4M leads reconcile to 2,17,266 sales — 9.05% end to end

62% of test-drivers never book — 4.0 lakh warm prospects a year. The contacted-to-showroom stage loses more in absolute terms (6.9 lakh), but test-drivers are the most expensive prospects Asteron owns, acquired twice over while marketing cost per sale rose 31%.

2

The pool nobody is counting

Customer care runs 5.8 million interactions a year at 66% first-contact resolution — roughly 2 million repeat contacts annually.

This is the most likely single driver of NPS 24 against a benchmark of 39, despite comparable product quality.

It is also the only large pool Asteron owns outright — no dealer boundary to negotiate. Lifting FCR to 80% deflects ~8 lakh repeat contacts and is the fastest Year 1 win available.

Where to play

We fund the top 20 cities, SUV and EV, digital-origin demand. We do not fund network-wide measurement of walk-in and referral volume — that business sits outside the metros, where share is holding.

Mobile + VIN as a join key lifts common-ID coverage from 38% to roughly 75% in metros within two quarters, so value does not wait on a two-year data programme.

Concentration beats coverage — a budget spread across 340 cities is not a strategy
3

How it reaches 620 dealers

  • Champions and squeezed performers → Wave 1 in the metros; the cash-starved go first because they need it most
  • Willing but weak → Wave 2 with hands-on support
  • Most of the 27% loss-making outlets are fixable on aftersales mix and cost-to-serve; only a small tail is structurally unviable
We commit to no net loss of city coverage — in India, reach is share. Consolidation only where two outlets cannibalise one catchment.

Vega, in 15 months, is our Year 2 proof point across 420 EV-ready outlets. EVs are 7% of sales today and perhaps 13% by Year 3, so service-revenue erosion is a Year 3+ design question, not a Year 1 risk.

Why the warm end

A 16-hour first response is an SLA failure before it is an intelligence failure — so the front door moves into the process bucket, with a dashboard and a dealer scorecard.

What the warm end needs is discrimination: which of 6.5 lakh test-drivers is finance-constrained, blocked on exchange value, or cross-shopping a named rival — inside the 48 hours where the decision is actually made.

The mechanism that replaces use-case funding

  • A single AI investment council with dealer representation — one standing portfolio, fixed quarterly review, no side doors
  • Owner, baseline and adoption target before rupee one — the direct inverse of 43 / 36 / 34
  • A frontline route from consultant and service advisor into the product backlog, not around it
  • Single-attribution ledger — one primary claim per outcome, verified quarterly
If a gate fails, budget is recycled inside the envelope, not rolled over — the discipline the last ₹225 cr never had.
3

What the jury will ask

Why will dealers adopt? Because Year 1 pays them first, through the profit pool they already depend on.

What if rivals move too? We commit to absolute share recovery of +1.8pp, not to a rank we do not control. If No. 3 also grows, we still close the experience gap that caused the loss.

Why only ₹250 cr? Because a third of the fix is process, and process is nearly free.

Both share and dealer margin must move, or the programme has failed

Sensitivity and risk

Market growth: at 5% annual PV growth the Year 3 unit gain rises to ~1.05 lakh and contribution to ~₹1,090 cr. Flat market is the base case by choice.

Competitive response: if No. 3 grows to 13.6%, we reach 13.0% and remain No. 4 — the commitment is absolute share, not rank.

  • Dealer resistance → aftersales first, co-investment tiers, archetype waves
  • Data below plan → metro-first bootstrap; agent scope narrows, it does not stop
  • Vega slips → Year 2 rests on warm-end conversion, not the launch
The largest risk is repeating the last programme — many initiatives, no verified value. The council and the gates exist to prevent exactly that.
